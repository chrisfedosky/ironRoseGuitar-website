`# Guitar Teaching Website — Content Guide`

`> Goal per page: answer one question, drive one action.`

`---`

`## HOME PAGE`

`**The question it answers:** "Is this the right teacher for me?"`

`**The action it drives:** Click to Lessons or Contact`

`This is your storefront window. Visitors decide in 5–8 seconds whether to stay.`

`You don't need to say everything — you need to say the *right* things fast.`

`### Sections (top to bottom)`

`**1. Hero**`

`The single most important block on the entire site.`

`- Headline: Lead with the *outcome*, not the service. Not "Guitar Lessons in [City]" but`

`something like "Learn guitar the way you actually want to play it" or`

`"Real songs. Real technique. No fluff." Keep it punchy — 6–10 words max.`

`- Sub-headline (1–2 sentences): Who you teach + what makes you different.`

`Example: "I teach beginners through advanced players in [City] and online —`

`acoustic, electric, fingerpicking, songwriting."`

`- CTA button: "See Lessons & Pricing" or "Book a Free Intro Lesson"`

`**2. Trust Bar (optional but fast)**`

`3–4 quick credibility signals in a single horizontal row.`

`` Examples: `15+ years teaching` · `All ages welcome` · `Beginner-friendly` · `Online & in-person` ``

`This takes 3 seconds to read and does a lot of work.`

`**3. "Is This For You?" / Who You Teach**`

`Name your students so they self-identify. Use 3–4 short cards or a brief list:`

`- Total beginners who've never held a guitar`

`- Kids (or adults — specify your range)`

`- People who tried before but quit`

`- Intermediate players who've plateaued`

`- Singer-songwriters who want to accompany themselves`

`**4. How It Works**`

`3 simple steps remove friction and make contacting you feel low-stakes.`

`Example:`

`1. Reach out — fill out the contact form (takes 2 minutes)`

`2. Free intro lesson — we figure out your goals and starting point`

`3. Start learning — weekly lessons tailored to you`

`**5. Social Proof**`

`Even 2–3 testimonials are powerful. Pull the most specific ones you have.`

`Vague: "Great teacher!" — Weak.`

`Specific: "I went from never touching a guitar to playing my first full song in 6 weeks." — Strong.`

`If you're just starting out, use a quote from a friend/family member you've taught, or skip`

`and add a "First lesson is free — no pressure" trust line instead.`

`**6. Featured Audio / Sample**`

`This is where your SoundCloud embed earns its place. One short clip (30–60 sec)`

`of you playing something compelling. Caption it: "Here's what we're working toward."`

`It makes you real. It makes students want that.`

`**7. Bottom CTA**`

`Repeat the main CTA from the hero. Don't make people scroll back up.`

`Add a low-pressure line: "No experience needed. First lesson free."`

`---`

`## LESSONS PAGE`

`**The question it answers:** "What exactly am I buying, and what will it cost?"`

`**The action it drives:** Contact / Book`

`This is your menu. Be specific. Vague pricing and vague lesson descriptions`

`lose students to teachers who just told them clearly what they'd get.`

`### Sections`

`**1. Page Header / Intro (2–3 sentences)**`

`Quick framing: What your lessons are like in general.`

`Example: "Lessons are structured around your goals — not a rigid curriculum.`

`Whether you want to play campfire songs or shred solos, we build a path that fits you."`

`**2. Lesson Formats**`

`Cover the logistics people need to know before they'll commit to contacting you.`

`- Duration: 30 min / 45 min / 60 min (and who each is good for)`

`- Format: In-person (location / your home studio or theirs) and/or Online (platform you use)`

`- Frequency: Weekly recommended, bi-weekly okay for casual learners`

`**3. Skill Levels**`

`Use 3 cards/sections — Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced — and for each one list:`

`- Who it's for (1 sentence description)`

`- What you'll cover (4–6 bullet points of actual content)`

`- Example milestone ("By end of month 3, most students can play a full song start to finish")`

`Example Beginner content bullets:`

`- How to hold the guitar and build calluses painlessly`

`- Reading chord diagrams and basic tablature`

`- Your first 5 chords (G, C, D, Em, Am)`

`- Strumming patterns and basic rhythm`

`- First full song`

`Example Intermediate bullets:`

`- Barre chords and power chords`

`- Pentatonic scale and intro to soloing`

`- Music theory you'll actually use (keys, the CAGED system)`

`- Playing along to recordings`

`**4. Styles / Genres You Teach**`

`List them explicitly. Students search for this.`

`Examples: Acoustic, Electric, Classical, Blues, Rock, Folk, Country, Fingerpicking,`

`Singer-Songwriter, Pop, Metal, Jazz (intro), Songwriting/Composition`

`**5. Pricing**`

`Be transparent. Hidden pricing is a conversion killer.`

`- Per-lesson rate(s) by duration`

`- Monthly package rate (if you offer one — encourages commitment)`

`- Note about first lesson: "First lesson is free — it's a chance to meet, set goals,`

`and make sure we're a good fit."`

`**6. Gear / Equipment**`

`Short note: "Don't have a guitar yet? No problem — I can make recommendations`

`before your first lesson." This removes a common objection from beginners.`

`**7. CTA**`

`"Ready to start? Get in touch →" linking to Contact page.`

`## ABOUT PAGE`

`**The question it answers:** "Can I trust this person? Will I enjoy learning from them?"`

`**The action it drives:** Builds enough trust to go contact or book`

`People hire *people*. This page is doing emotional work, not just listing credentials.`

`Show your personality. Be human. Students will spend an hour a week with you —`

`they want to know if they'll like you.`

`### Sections`

`**1. Opening Paragraph — Your "Why"**`

`Not a resume intro. Tell the story of why you teach.`

`Example: "I picked up my first guitar at 13 because I wanted to play Nirvana songs —`

`and promptly struggled for 6 months with no direction. When I finally had a good teacher,`

`everything clicked. That's the experience I want to give every student."`

`**2. Musical Background**`

`- When you started playing`

`- How you developed (self-taught, formal training, both?)`

`- Styles and genres you've played and performed`

`- Any notable performances, bands, recordings (keep it brief, not braggy)`

`- Instruments you play beyond guitar (if any — shows musicianship)`

`**3. Teaching Background**`

`- How long you've been teaching`

`- Types of students you've worked with (ages, levels)`

`- Any formal training in music education (if applicable)`

`- Teaching philosophy in 2–3 sentences:`

`What do you believe about how people learn best?`

`Example: "I don't believe in years of scales before you're allowed to have fun.`

`We play songs from lesson one — technique builds in context."`

`**4. Personal / Human Detail**`

`One short paragraph that makes you a real person.`

`Mention: where you're based, maybe one non-guitar interest, a favorite band or album,`

`something that shows your taste. Students want to know if your musical sensibility matches theirs.`

`**5. Photo**`

`Essential. Candid with a guitar beats a formal headshot.`

`Caption it with something warm, not just your name.`

`**6. CTA at bottom**`

`"Want to learn more about how I teach? Check out the FAQ →" or`

`"Ready to get started? I'd love to hear from you →" (link to Contact)`

`## FAQ PAGE`

`**The question it answers:** "I have one last concern — can you address it?"`

`**The action it drives:** Removes the final objection before contacting`

`People who reach the FAQ page are interested but have a specific hesitation.`

`Don't write essays. Write scannable Q&A pairs — short questions, direct answers.`

`Group them into 3–4 loose categories.`

`### Category: Getting Started`

`- **Do I need my own guitar to start?**`

`No — your first lesson can happen with any guitar, borrowed or rented.`

`I'll give you specific recommendations before you buy anything.`

`- **What age do you teach?**`

`[Your actual answer — be specific. e.g., "Ages 7 and up through adults.`

`For kids under 10, ukulele is often a better starting point — I can discuss that with you."]`

`- **Do I need to know how to read music?**`

`No. Most guitar is learned through tablature (guitar-specific notation) and by ear.`

`I'll introduce music reading gradually if that's a goal for you.`

`- **I tried learning before and gave up — is that a problem?**`

`Not at all. Most adult beginners have tried before. The difference is usually`

`having a structured path and someone to hold you accountable.`

`### Category: Lessons & Format`

`- **What happens in a typical lesson?**`

`We usually start with a quick warm-up, review what you practiced, work on`

`something new, and end with a clear practice plan for the week.`

`- **How long are lessons?**`

`30, 45, or 60 minutes. 30 min works well for young children.`

`Most students prefer 45–60 min once they have material to work through.`

`- **Do you teach online?**`

`Yes. Online lessons work well and I teach students across [region/country].`

`You'll need a device with a camera, decent lighting, and an okay internet connection.`

`- **How often should I take lessons?**`

`Weekly is ideal, especially in the first few months. Bi-weekly works for`

`casual learners or people with packed schedules.`

`### Category: Progress & Practice`

`- **How long until I can play a real song?**`

`Most beginners play their first complete song in 4–8 lessons. The timeline`

`depends on practice between lessons — even 15 minutes a day makes a big difference.`

`- **How much should I practice between lessons?**`

`15–30 minutes a day is plenty for beginners. More is great, but consistency`

`beats marathon sessions. I'll always give you a clear practice plan.`

`- **Do you assign homework?**`

`I give you a specific practice focus after every lesson — not a list of exercises,`

`but clear targets: "Be able to switch between G and C without looking by next week."`

`### Category: Logistics & Pricing`

`- **How much do lessons cost?**`

`[Link to or repeat your pricing from the Lessons page. Don't make them hunt.]`

`- **What's your cancellation policy?**`

`[Your actual policy — be fair and be clear.]`

`- **How do I schedule my first lesson?**`

`Fill out the contact form — I'll get back to you within [X hours/1 business day]`

`to set up your free intro lesson.`

`## CONTACT PAGE`

`**The question it answers:** "How do I reach out — and will it be awkward?"`

`**The action it drives:** Submit the form`

`The contact page has one job: make reaching out feel easy and low-stakes.`

`Keep it simple. The biggest mistake is a form that feels like a job application.`

`### Sections`

`**1. Opener (2–3 sentences, warm and direct)**`

`Set expectations and make it feel easy.`

`Example: "Fill out the form below and I'll get back to you within one business day.`

`The first lesson is free — no commitment, just a chance to meet and figure out your goals."`

`**2. The Form (Netlify Forms)**`

`Keep fields minimal. Every extra field costs you a submission.`

`Required:`

`- Name`

`- Email`

`- Message / Tell me about yourself (open text — pre-fill placeholder with a prompt:`

`"e.g., I'm a total beginner, I want to learn acoustic guitar, I'm free evenings...")`

`Optional (adds context without friction):`

`- Phone (optional — for people who prefer a call)`

`- How did you hear about me? (dropdown: Google, friend referral, social media, other)`

`**DO NOT add:** skill level dropdowns, lesson preference fields, scheduling widgets`

`on first contact. That's for *after* they respond. Keep the barrier to entry low.`

`**3. What Happens Next (below the form)**`

`A short 2-step note that removes anxiety:`

`1. I'll reply within [X timeframe] to say hello and set up your free intro lesson.`

`2. The intro lesson is 30 minutes — no charge, no obligation.`

`**4. Alternative Contact (optional)**`

`If you're comfortable: your email address written out (good for accessibility and trust)`

`and/or: "Prefer to text? [Phone number]"`

`This builds trust even if people don't use it — it signals you're a real, reachable person.`

`**5. DO NOT include:**`

`- A full FAQ (that's what the FAQ page is for)`

`- Long bios or lesson descriptions`

`- Anything that delays the form or distracts from it`

`---`

`## Cross-Page Principles`

`**One CTA per page.** Don't offer "Contact / Book / See Lessons / Read FAQ" all at once`

`on the same section. Pick the one that fits where the visitor is in their decision.`

`**Repeat the free intro lesson offer everywhere.** It's your lowest-friction entry point`

`and your biggest conversion tool. Put it in the hero, the lessons page, and the contact page.`

`**Don't bury pricing.** Students comparison-shop. Hiding your pricing loses you students`

`to teachers who just told them. A visible price also filters out bad-fit inquiries.`

`**Testimonials belong on Home and Lessons** — where buying decisions are being made —`

`not just on a standalone "testimonials" page nobody visits.`

`**Bento box tip:** Each bento cell should carry one message or one piece of evidence.`

`If a cell needs more than 2–3 sentences to explain itself, it's probably two cells.`
